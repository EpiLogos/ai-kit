# Writing Rubric — Fault Catalogue & Audit Protocol

> **What this is.** The enforcement layer that binds the two existing references into the drafting loop. It is not a third style guide. `authentic-voice-reference.md` (Frank's voice: 31 excerpts, voice signature, edges) and `writing-style-reference.md` (Gebser, Jung, Dyczkowski, Hillman, Whitehead, Abram, Bergson, Bohm — with craft diagnoses and ten cross-cutting patterns) describe what good looks like. This file describes what failure looks like — with real failures and Frank's real corrections as the calibration data — and specifies the process that catches failure before Frank has to.
>
> **Why it exists.** In July 2026 a full draft of the Definition of God essay was produced without loading the references, in one continuous push, with no adversarial pass. Frank's inline [F] annotations on that draft identified a consistent fault-set. The references failed to prevent this not because they are weak but because (a) nothing forced them into context at drafting time, and (b) they are virtue-catalogues — a writer under drafting momentum rationalises compliance. Catching defects requires a fault-catalogue and a separate pass by eyes that didn't write the text.

---

## 0. Pre-flight — mandatory before drafting a word

1. Read `authentic-voice-reference.md` §IV (Voice Signature) and §V (Edges to Watch).
2. Read the **Cross-Cutting Patterns** section at the end of `writing-style-reference.md`. Read in full any excerpt relevant to the section's subject matter (e.g. Dyczkowski for Śaiva material, Jung for archetypal material, Whitehead for process material).
3. Read the three nearest paragraphs of Frank's own text to the passage being drafted or extended. New prose continues *those* paragraphs, not the model's defaults.
4. Build a **dependency map** for the section: for each claim, name where the reader received the materials to assess it. Anything not yet given is either taught in place or moved.
5. Draft. Then run §2 (mechanical sweep) and §3 (audit protocol). **No prose ships from a single pass.**

## 1. The Fault Catalogue

Each fault below is a degenerate form of a legitimate move. The rubric's job is to preserve the distinction, not to ban the move — a rubric that bans negation, aphorism, and abstraction outright would flatten the voice into timidity, which is just AI-slop with better manners. For each: the fault, the caught instance, Frank's correction verbatim, the repair, and the earned form of the same move.

### F1 — Copula fraud
**The fault.** Asserting "X is Y" between a notation and a concept, or between two terms of art, where the real content is an *operation* or *correspondence with a degree*. The copula performs insight instead of building it.

**Caught:** "The slash is icchā. … desire *is* the relation."
**[F]:** *"you're just doing 'x is y', 'the slash is ichaa', when thats not fucking true, the example expresses what the operation of the slash is doing, it reveals it to a degree… 'desire is the relation', what do you MEAN?"*

**Repair.** Rewrite the identity as the operation it compresses, with warrant and degree stated: *performs*, *operates as*, *insofar as* (Eckhart's *in quantum* is the essay's own register for this). A bare "is" is permitted only after the work is done — the identity derived or argued in place, its register named.

**Earned form.** Dyczkowski on vimarśa: the root *mṛṣ* is unpacked (touch, feel, understand, perceive, reflect, examine), then the operational definition — "the power of consciousness by virtue of which it can understand or perceive itself" — and only then does identity-language arrive. The "is" lands because the operation was shown first.

### F2 — Strawman negative / reflexive antithesis
**The fault.** "It is not X but Y" where nobody holds X. The negation is fake work — clearing ground that was never occupied. Includes openers of the form "it is not what [invented opponent] assumes."

**Caught:** "it is not an inert stroke of punctuation"; "because it is not what moral instinct assumes."
**[F]:** *"absolute waffle opening; just another strawman negative that sets up a shit point"* · *"what does moral instinct assume? you're the one assuming you can just point here and there lazily and this count as good prose."*

**Repair.** Apply `comparative-and-negation-gate.md`. A negation may target a position actually held, distinguish two defined operations, protect a warranted claim boundary, or disclose a formal/apophatic limit. Otherwise state the operative claim directly. Every retained comparison bears four burdens: identify its referent; specify the difference; establish its local relevance; and add information that would be lost if the negative clause were deleted. Budget check: if "not X but Y" appears more than once or twice per section, the rhythm has probably become a tic even where each sentence can be defended alone.

**Earned form.** The Negative Definition That Illuminates (cross-cutting pattern 2): "Deprived of reflective awareness, the light of consciousness would amount to no more than the mere appearing of phenomena unknown to anyone, like the light that shines reflected in a common mirror." The negation maps territory that genuinely resists direct predication. Test: does the negative *add* content, or only defer it?

### F3 — Nouned abstraction leaned on
**The fault.** Coining or borrowing an abstraction ("the anatomy of the miss," "the two throws," "structure," "the orbit") and then leaning on it as if established — hiding behind the noun to avoid explaining the dynamic in context.

**Caught:** "the anatomy of the miss"; leaning on "structure"; "orbit"-language left disembodied.
**[F]:** *"'anatomy of the miss' is classic shit you think sounds clever or deep but simply isn't… stop nouning things!!!"* · *"leaning on the word 'structure' seems to obviate you actually pointing to what the structure is"* · *"hiding behind a noun to get out of explaining a dynamic in context."*

**Repair.** Every abstraction doing load-bearing work is unpacked where it stands, or is one of Frank's established coinages. Prefer verbs: say what does what to what. If a noun-phrase was invented three sentences ago, it may not yet be used as a premise.

**Earned form.** The Earned Abstraction (cross-cutting pattern 4): concrete material first, the name after — "make the reader FEEL the weight before naming it." Frank's own move: "camps of and for our concentration" — the abstraction is cracked open in the same breath that coins it.

### F4 — The dump (sequence inversion)
**The fault.** Asserting compressed material — a matheme, a schema, a set of correspondences — before the reader has been given what's needed to read it, so that the explanation arrives *after* the assertion it should have preceded.

**Caught:** the two-mathemes block stated cold, explained one paragraph later.
**[F]:** *"we're giving all this as a dump, not really explaining it well, and if i were to read this i'd be going, this is nonsense… look at the next paragraph, it goes into what you just throw out up here without context."*

**Repair.** The dependency map (§0.4) is the check. Derive in place what can be derived in place; defer what cannot, with a plain promissory note naming its destination — and only once the local point stands complete without it.

**Earned form.** Whitehead's aeroplane: ground of particular observation, then the flight of generalization, then the landing — "the passage earns its own generality by performing the movement it describes."

### F5 — Tour-guide narration
**The fault.** The writer staging the essay instead of working in it: "Note the exactness of the mechanism," "Watch the machinery, not the chase," "Hold that leaf; it will be needed later," "the reader is owed the announcement."

**Caught:** all of the above, plus their siblings in every unannotated section.
**[F]:** *"you're narrating the meaning like a story, can you write this as if you're working with actual truths?"*

**Repair.** Delete the narrator. State the fact; the structure does the pointing. Forward references, where genuinely needed, are made in the working voice ("the derivation below shows…") — never the showman's.

**Earned form.** The Primary Source Allowed to Speak (cross-cutting pattern 9): at the moments of greatest weight, step aside and let the material work. Frank's stance in the methodological aside: "integral stenography, faithfully reporting" — servant of the material, not its compère.

### F6 — Deployed erudition without teaching
**The fault.** Technical concepts from another domain used as texture — chaos theory, set theory, Śaiva anatomy — without being taught, and without warrant in the text for entering that domain at all.

**Caught:** strange attractors and basins used unexplained; the Śakti triad arriving from nowhere.
**[F]:** *"might wanna more clearly explain what these chaos theory concepts mean"* · *"all the stuff on the shaktis again has little to no context, you give no warrant for why we go there."*

**Repair.** Every imported concept gets its two or three sentences in place — what it is, what it does, why here — before it carries weight. The warrant for crossing into another tradition's vocabulary is stated in the text, not assumed. The standing instruction from CLAUDE.md applies to all registers: **teach as it reads.**

**Earned form.** Dyczkowski again — the root taught before the term is used. Frank's "de-finition" — the etymology taught inside the clause that uses it, no downtime.

### F7 — Ungenerous openings
**The fault.** Section openings that gesture at an abstraction instead of opening the horizon — giving the what without the why, leaving the reader to reconstruct the stakes.

**Caught:** the Images-section opening ("Where direct representation fails, structure gathers.").
**[F]:** *"this whole section needs to be more properly introductory bud, seems like we're kinda failing to open the horizon up in a way that's generous enough to give a sense of not just what but why we're going down this path."*

**Repair.** Every section opening does three jobs: receives what came before; names why this path, now; opens onto the actual subject — a thing, a scene, a fact — not an abstraction awaiting definition.

### F8 — Claudeism cadence and sloganisation
**The fault.** The recognisable machine rhythms: sentence subjects that are disembodied "the + abstraction"; the "not this but that" pulse as default music; aphorism-shaped closers performing depth instead of arriving at it; prose chopped into small bounded passages that break the organism-sentence rhythm; slogans repeated until they die ("a circle drawn in reality…").

**Caught:** across the draft.
**[F]:** *"you're getting into all the 'claudeisms' which just bury any possible meaning or value to the ideas under horrible sentence structure, again leading so often with a disembodied 'the' and so much 'not this but that'… you have examples everywhere of my writing, what's so hard about this?"* · *"we're going into that region of sloganisation with the circle drawn thing"* · *"you also decided to break up the prose into small bounded little passages, not sure this retains or harmonises as structurally sound across the whole piece."*

**Repair.** Sentence subjects are the actual agents and things of the argument. Antithesis only where both terms do work. An aphorism is an *arrival* — the compression of an argument just completed (never an opener, never a substitute). Paragraphs are movements: merge fragments; sustain the architecturally complex sentence that accretes clauses "the way a crystal grows" (Voice Signature 8). A slogan that has appeared once may be *developed* (Cusa's sphere developing the circle image) but not repeated as incantation.

**Earned form.** "The negative judgment is the peak of mentality" — full force because it lands *inside* a methodological argument. Frank: "The 0 is the One, the 1 is the All! Thus the All is none, once and for all." — an entire matheme arriving as a pun; and "Waking up is just a matter of getting tired / Of all the bullshit" — the sudden drop that tests whether the thought survives at ground level (Voice Signature 2).

### F9 — Listing under compression (the gravitational pull of comprehensiveness)
**The fault.** Multiple names, myths, or technical terms compressed into a short span such that each receives only a pointer — a truncated gesture rather than genuine explanation or derivation of relevance — because the passage is trying to cover too much ground in too little room. Distinct from F6: F6 is one imported term deployed with *no* teaching at all; F9 is several references each getting *some* gloss, but too little to show why this reference, here, is doing real work — the paragraph reads as a catalogue even though no single item is technically bare. This is Edge 1 (`authentic-voice-reference.md` §V) — "the voice wants to include everything... some passages accumulate so many cross-references that the central insight gets buried under its own evidence" — promoted from a pre-flight caution into an actual audit gate.

**Caught:** "Nāgārjuna built that path formally" (Definition of God, Draft 3) — a bare name-check assuming the figure and his period are already known.
**[F]:** *"we just drop 'Nāgārjuna built that path formally', but may need to say 'buddhist sage, did this at this time etc'"* — even one clause of dating and school-affiliation was the missing teaching, not a rewrite of the sentence's content.
**Also caught:** a paragraph stacking five to six aphoristic tags across as many mythic references in a single paragraph (Śiva/Pārvatī, alchemical Rebis, Isis/Osiris, Inanna, the taijitu) — most individually glossed, but the density risks burying the paragraph's actual payoff line under its own evidence, per Edge 1 exactly.

**Repair.** For any paragraph naming more than a small handful of external references, ask: is each one doing *Argued* work (something the essay's own structural claims depend on) or *Offered* work (contemplative fit, explicitly licensed as such)? If Argued, it needs real derivation — why this reference, here, matters — not a name-check. If Offered, the compression is licensed only when the passage has *honestly marked itself as Offered* before the list arrives (see Earned form) — never smuggled through at Argued-strength brevity.

**Earned form.** A section that needs to move fast through many names says so, in its own voice, first: "The schema that follows is offered, not derived — a reading of the record, not a theorem" (Definition of God, "The Winds Round the One Torus," preceding its own dense catalogue of civilisational parallels). The reader is told which register they're in before the compression starts, so the speed is licensed rather than assumed. Contrast with Cross-Cutting Pattern 9 (the primary source allowed to speak): one myth given the room to actually work (Ovid's Apollo and Daphne, several sentences) versus five myths given a clause each — the difference between earned depth and listing is room, not correctness.

## 2. Mechanical sweep — run after drafting, before audit

Greps generate candidates, not verdicts: every hit is rewritten or explicitly justified in the ship-note.

```bash
# reflexive antithesis and corrective negation
rg -n --pcre2 '(?i)\b(?:is|are|was|were|does|do|did|can|could|means?)\s+not\b[^.!?\n]{0,140}\bbut\b|\bnot\s+(?:merely|simply|only|just)\b|\bnot\s+as\b[^.!?\n]{0,140}\bbut\s+as\b' <path>
rg -n --pcre2 '^(?:This|That|It|The [^.!?]{1,80})\s+(?:is|are|was|were|does|do|means?)\s+not\b' <path>
# throat-clearing imperatives & narrator
rg -n 'must (now )?be (stated|read|said|named|understood)|[Nn]ote th|[Ww]atch |[Hh]old that|[Rr]ecall |the reader|this essay' <path>
# disembodied-the openers (candidates)
rg -n '^(But|And|Now|Yet|So) |^The [a-z]+ (of|that|which)' <path>
# simile quota (cap ~2 per section)
rg -c 'the way a|the way the' <path>
# copula candidates between terms of art (adjust terms per piece)
rg -n ' is (the|a|an) [a-z]+\.$' <path>
```

Manual checks the greps can't do: sub-eight-word paragraph closers (aphorism candidates — earned or performed?); paragraph lengths (fragmentation); per-section "not X but Y" count; F9 listing-under-compression (count distinct external references per paragraph — myths, thinkers, technical terms — and check each against gate 9 by hand; density alone isn't greppable, only judgeable).

## 3. Audit protocol — the fresh-eyes pass

The writer cannot audit prose it just wrote; it rationalises. Each drafted or revised section goes to a **fresh agent** with no memory of having written it. Spawn-prompt (use verbatim, attach the section text):

> You are auditing a section of philosophical prose against `writing-guidance-tools/WRITING-RUBRIC.md` and `writing-guidance-tools/comparative-and-negation-gate.md`. Read both in full, then read `authentic-voice-reference.md` §IV–V and the Cross-Cutting Patterns at the end of `writing-style-reference.md`. You did not write this text; your job is to refuse it. For every paragraph, answer the nine gate-questions in rubric §4 and flag each violation with: line reference, fault code (F1–F9), the quoted phrase, and a one-sentence repair direction. For each comparative or negative candidate, classify it as `rewrite`, `sourced disagreement`, `operational distinction`, `claim boundary`, `formal negation`, or `verified quotation`. Do not rewrite passages. Do not praise. If a flagged construction is in fact an earned form, pass it and say in one line why. End with a count by fault code and classification.

Revise; re-audit if the flag count was high; only then deliver. **Deliver with a ship-note**: what moved, what was rewritten, every mechanical-sweep hit that was kept and why, and any departure from an [F] annotation with its reason — so Frank's spot-checks are cheap and departures are visible rather than smuggled.

## 4. Semantic gates — the auditor's nine questions per paragraph

1. **Warrant** — has the reader received the materials for every claim here? If not, where are they?
2. **Copula** — does each "is" between terms of art assert an earned identity? In which register — derived, argued, or offered?
3. **Negation** — does each negative target a position actually held, and does it add content?
4. **Abstraction** — is each load-bearing noun unpacked in place or already established?
5. **Teaching** — is each imported technical term taught before it carries weight, with its warrant stated?
6. **Opening** — if this begins a section: does it receive, give the why, and open onto the actual subject?
7. **Voice** — read the three nearest authorial paragraphs: is this prose continuous with them in rhythm, register, and sentence-architecture?
8. **Aphorism** — does each compressed line arrive from an argument, or perform in place of one?
9. **Enumeration** — if this paragraph names more than a small handful of external references (myths, thinkers, technical terms), has each received room to show its relevance, or is the paragraph substituting the comprehensiveness of the list for the derivation of any single item? Is the compression honestly marked as Offered-register before it starts, or does it smuggle Argued-strength dependence through Offered-brevity?

## 6. Enforcement wiring

1. **Auto-load:** `Antykathera-Essay-Work/CLAUDE.md` names `writing-guidance-tools/README.md` as the governing entry point. The rule applies to the plan, argument and section nodes, source-record commentary, captions, plates, and submission prose.
2. **Local authority:** this directory is the essay's authoritative copy. Audit prompts and project rules link here rather than to a copy elsewhere.
3. **Hard gates:** no single-pass prose; no shipping without the comparison/negation sweep, adversarial audit, and ship-note.
4. **Preservation gate:** verified quotations, bibliographic forms, raw/imported texts, OCR witnesses, and frozen legacy provenance are read but never silently normalised to satisfy the rubric.
5. **Honest limit:** a rubric catches defects; it does not generate taste. The generative side comes from the pre-flight reads and from continuing Frank's paragraphs; the final arbiter of whether a sentence lets the idea through is Frank's rasa. When in doubt: preserve his phrasing, propose rather than replace.
