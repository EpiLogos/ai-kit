# Writing Laws

These laws are the small, generative layer above the audit rubric. They adapt the clarity disciplines of ASD-STE100 and George Orwell to philosophical prose without turning that prose into a maintenance manual.

## Scope

ASD-STE100 is a controlled language for technical documentation. Its strongest transferable principles are controlled meaning, explicit agents and actions, gradual presentation, stable terminology, and one governing topic at a time. Its vocabulary controls and sentence or paragraph caps do **not** govern literary-philosophical work.

Orwell's six rules oppose stale figures, needless length, removable words, avoidable passives, unnecessary jargon, and obedience that produces barbarous prose. The sixth rule governs the other five: clarity serves truth; rules do not replace judgment.

## The Seven Laws

### L1 — Meaning before wording

Know what the sentence claims, what warrants it, and how strongly it may be stated. No rhythm, metaphor, terminology, or schema may substitute for that work.

### L2 — Put actors in subjects and actions in verbs

Prefer the thing that acts as the grammatical subject and its action as the verb. Use passive construction when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, deliberately withheld, or when the receiver of the action is the real subject.

### L3 — Give before use

Present prerequisites before conclusions, operations before identities, and explanations before compressed forms. Let each sentence govern one intelligible movement and each paragraph develop one argumentative movement. Complexity may accumulate inside that movement.

### L4 — Make terms answer to referents

Use one term consistently for one referent within a register. When a word changes sense, discipline, scale, or status—from image to claim, for example—announce and warrant the shift.

### L5 — Make difficulty earn itself

Use a technical term, foreign word, etymology, neologism, or figure only when it carries meaning ordinary language would lose. Teach it before it bears weight. Use no stale image merely because it arrives ready-made.

### L6 — Cut dead work; preserve living complexity

Remove words, negations, stage directions, repetitions, citations, and transitions that do no semantic work. Keep length, recursion, ambiguity, or syntactic difficulty when the thought requires them and the reader can follow their movement.

### L7 — Truth and voice outrank the laws

Break any law rather than falsify the thought, flatten an earned voice, or produce ugly and lifeless prose. The exception must be articulable: name what the apparent violation achieves and why a compliant alternative would lose it.
