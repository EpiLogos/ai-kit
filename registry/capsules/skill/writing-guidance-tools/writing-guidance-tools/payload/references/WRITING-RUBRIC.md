# Writing Rubric

Use this rubric after reading `WRITING-LAWS.md`. It tests whether the draft carries its argument and voice. It is not a generator, a style template, or a list of forbidden strings.

## Review method

1. Read the whole section in argumentative context.
2. State its governing claim and the work of each paragraph.
3. Audit every paragraph against G1–G10 with calibration files still closed.
4. Record candidate faults without rewriting.
5. Calibrate only candidates that remain ambiguous.
6. Revise meaning and sequence before diction; then re-audit changed paragraphs.

Use this flag form:

`location · fault code · exact phrase · failure · repair direction`

Patterns, counts, and software checks may help a reviewer relocate prose already judged suspect. They cannot establish a fault.

## Semantic gates

### G1 — Claim and warrant

What does the paragraph claim? Has the essay supplied enough evidence or reasoning for the strength of that claim?

### G2 — Actor and operation

Can the reader tell what acts, what changes, and upon what? Does a noun or copula conceal the actual operation?

### G3 — Sequence and dependency

Did the paragraph give the reader each prerequisite before using it? Does compression arrive after derivation rather than before it?

### G4 — Governing movement

Does every sentence advance one intelligible movement, and does the paragraph develop one movement without becoming a dump, catalogue, or set of slogan fragments?

### G5 — Term and referent

Does each load-bearing term keep a stable referent and register? Is every shift in meaning or status marked and warranted?

### G6 — Difficulty and teaching

Does each technical, foreign, etymological, neologistic, or figurative construction earn its place, receive enough teaching, and enter for a stated reason?

### G7 — Negation and opposition

Does each negative answer a real position or define a real boundary? Do both sides of an antithesis carry content?

### G8 — Opening and transition

Does a section opening receive what precedes it, show why this path matters now, and open onto its actual subject? Do transitions perform logical work rather than narrate the essay?

### G9 — Compression and enumeration

Does an aphorism arrive from work already done? Does each cited figure, myth, or parallel contribute enough to justify its inclusion? Is offered material distinguished from argued material?

### G10 — Voice and exception

Is the prose continuous with the essay's own achieved voice? Is an apparent breach of a law a deliberate, explainable gain, or an unearned mannerism?

## Fault catalogue

### F1 — Copula fraud

An identity between terms performs insight that the prose has not established. Expose the operation, degree, or register before compressing it into “is.”

### F2 — Reflexive antithesis

“Not X but Y” clears ground nobody occupied or creates rhythm without thought. Name the real opposed position or state the positive claim.

### F3 — Nouned abstraction

A coined or borrowed noun conceals what happens and then becomes a premise. Restore actors and verbs; establish the abstraction before leaning on it.

### F4 — Sequence inversion

A schema, technical claim, or conclusion arrives before the material needed to read it. Teach or derive first; defer honestly when necessary.

### F5 — Tour-guide narration

The writer tells the reader to note, watch, hold, or remember instead of making the structure perceptible. Remove the compère and perform the transition.

### F6 — Imported erudition

A technical term, foreign phrase, etymology, tradition, or scientific analogy supplies prestige or texture without teaching and local warrant.

### F7 — Ungenerous opening

A section begins with atmospheric abstraction but withholds its stakes, subject, or relation to what precedes it.

### F8 — Machine cadence

Predictable antithesis, disembodied abstract subjects, slogan-shaped closers, uniform paragraph units, or repeated syntactic templates perform depth.

### F9 — Listing under compression

Names, myths, citations, or technical terms accumulate faster than the paragraph can establish their relevance, burying the claim beneath evidence-shaped material.

### F10 — Prefabricated language

A stale figure, stock transition, jargon bundle, or ready-made phrase arrives in place of a freshly perceived relation. Replace it with literal precision or an image that belongs to this thought.

## Repair order

Repair in this order:

1. false or unsupported meaning;
2. missing warrant and dependency;
3. unstable terms or registers;
4. paragraph movement;
5. sentence agency and operation;
6. cadence and diction.

Never solve a semantic fault with cosmetic variation. Never flatten a correct, taught, and voice-bearing construction merely because it is long, passive, technical, figurative, or unusual.
