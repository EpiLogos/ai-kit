# Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM): what the evidence supports

> Generated research, 27 October 2026, and not yet reviewed by the owner. A research pass read Google's help pages and blog, the Enterprise API documentation, the unofficial clients' repositories, papers and analogue projects. Most pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so quotations are paraphrases unless a source is cited for the exact words. Every claim carries its firmness: documented, measured, reverse-engineered or inference.
>
> The design that uses it is [`../v2/25-SOURCE-TO-INSIGHT.md`](../v2/25-SOURCE-TO-INSIGHT.md). Glade's earlier API research (`Work/projects/Glade/docs/RESEARCH-GEMINI-NOTEBOOK-API.md`, 8 August 2026) reached the same conclusions as the API section below: no consumer API, an Enterprise v1alpha Preview that provisions but cannot query, and the same limits.

---


Gemini Notebook (renamed from NotebookLM on 16 July 2026) is a notebook-scoped source-to-insight system: a sealed set of sources, a chat grounded only in the checked subset, and a Studio of generators that turn the same sources into audio, video, slides, reports and more. Google documents the contract and the product surface in detail but has never described the answering pipeline: chunk size, embeddings, ranking and the point where it stops stuffing the 1M-token window and starts retrieving are all undisclosed, and the only first-party retrieval sentence says that with many sources it "retrieves the most relevant information" ([Help FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en)). Reverse-engineering of the app's RPCs shows that citations are structured objects (source id, verbatim fragment, character offsets, score), which supports the inference that they are pointers into stored source text rather than quotes the model writes; this is the most reproducible idea in the product. Independent evidence that the quality beats competitors is thin: one preprint with 15 responses per arm found hallucinations in **13%** of Notebook responses against **40%** for ChatGPT and for Gemini 2.5 Pro ([arXiv 2509.25498](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25498v1)), small clinical studies found 86 to 93% accuracy with errors mostly omissions, and no ablation isolates which factor matters. Studio formats share one recipe (source ids, preset, length, language, focus prompt, asynchronous job, stored prompt), but grounding varies by format and slide revisions explicitly ignore sources. The programmatic surface is thin: there is no consumer API, the Enterprise v1alpha API can create notebooks, load sources and start one audio overview but cannot ask a question, and the popular unofficial clients use browser cookies against private RPCs and carry their own Terms-of-Service warnings. For a design of your own, copy the closed-corpus contract, the span-addressed source store, per-query source scope, notes-as-sources and the Studio recipe; route between full context and retrieval and measure on your own corpus; and treat Notebook as a disposable renderer, never as the system of record.

## A notebook is a sealed source set that compounds only through notes

Gemini Notebook is what the owner calls Notebook. Google renamed NotebookLM on 16 July 2026 with a new logo, says it "remains a standalone product," and keeps old shared links working through redirects ([Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/07/notebooklm-now-gemini-notebook.html)). Help pages exist under both names, and the Google Cloud enterprise edition still carries "NotebookLM" in its IAM roles and URL paths ([Enterprise docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/overview)). Four tags mark how firm a claim is throughout this report. *Documented* means Google (a help page, blog, Cloud doc, or a named team member) said it. *Measured* means an independent test found it. *Reverse-engineered* means it was derived from network or Android-app analysis, chiefly the unofficial notebooklm-py project. *Inference* means the researchers reasoned to it from those. Most help pages were read through a summarising fetch tool and notebooklm-py through its docs rather than its source files, so wording is sometimes paraphrased.

The object model is simple and documented. A notebook is "a collection of sources for a specific project" with three panels, Sources, Chat and Studio, and the checkboxes in Sources decide which sources the model uses for chat and for generation ([Create a notebook](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16206563?hl=en)). Isolation is explicit: "Each notebook is independent. Gemini Notebook can't access information across multiple notebooks at the same time" (same page). Scope behaves as a hard filter: sources are "always used in either the entire set or the subset you select," notes only when selected, and conversation history always ([Help FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en)); the reverse-engineered chat request carries an explicit list of source ids ([chat.proto](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/src/notebooklm/_android/proto_src/google/internal/labs/tailwind/orchestration/v1/chat.proto)). Unchecked sources still count toward limits but are not referenced. The unofficial tmc/nlm client warns that a question sent without pinned ids uses server-side state, so answers change as the notebook grows ([tmc/nlm](https://github.com/tmc/nlm)), a drift hazard a new design should remove by storing the scope with every answer.

Users pool sources ad hoc by importing into the notebook and then toggling subsets. Fast Research searches the web or Drive and imports only the results the user ticks; Deep Research runs for a few minutes and returns a report plus cited and uncited sources to choose from ([Add or discover sources](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en)). Since 8 June 2026, agentic chat can start from a loose question, search the web and build the collection while the user approves each addition ([Google blog, 8 June 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/notebooklm/better-research-notebooklm/)). With five or more sources the notebook auto-labels them by topic, and labels are editable ([Add or discover sources](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en)). The pool is capped per notebook at 50, 100, 300, 500 or 600 sources depending on plan, with 100 to 500 notebooks per user ([Plans](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16213268?hl=en)). Moving material between notebooks is limited to copying: "Copy notebook" duplicates sources and Studio content but not chat history or notes ([Create a notebook](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16206563?hl=en)), and reverse-engineered RPCs copy sources and artifacts between notebooks ([rpc-reference](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/rpc-reference.md)). The community jacob-bd client advertises "cross-notebook queries" ([jacob-bd](https://github.com/jacob-bd/gemini-notebook-mcp-cli)); the service documents no such operation, so this is presumably client-side fan-out (*inference*).

The limits of isolation show in how Google bridges it. Notebooks inside the Gemini app, launched 8 April 2026 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/04/08/gemini-app-notebooks/)), sync with Gemini Notebook, and users can opt in to treat Gemini chats as shared context across notebooks, read-only inside Notebook. Answers are grounded only in the notebook's sources in Gemini Notebook but may also use web search and tools in Gemini; Gemini cannot create Studio artifacts; and a notebook containing Gemini chats cannot be shared ([Notebooks in Gemini Apps](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/17003757?hl=en)). Workspace users can also add a notebook as a source inside Gemini ([Workspace Updates, Jan 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/01/take-notebooks-further-notebooklm-gemini.html)). Google's route around isolation is therefore context sharing and notebook-as-source, not multi-notebook retrieval (*inference*), and reviewers keep naming the gap: "your knowledge doesn't accumulate across notebooks" ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/notebooklm-works-great-but-not-as-knowledge-base)).

The one built-in compounding path is the note. "Save to note" pins a chat answer with its tables and clickable citations; older Studio-panel notes must be converted, singly or all at once, before they count for Q&A, artifacts and citations; and a converted note appears in Sources with a checkbox and citation chips to the specific note passage ([Notes](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16262519?hl=en)). The client library refuses to save an answer as a note when it has no citations ([python-api](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/python-api.md)). The loop output, note, source, next output is the product's only documented way for synthesis to become citable material (*inference*). Sharing is role-based (Viewer, Editor), chat history stays private per user, a "Chat View" link hides sources and artifacts, and a recipient's copy keeps Studio content and sources but not notes or chat ([Create a notebook](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16206563?hl=en)).

## Ingestion flattens every source to text under per-source caps

Google documents the inputs precisely. Supported sources are PDF, Word, TXT, Markdown, CSV, PowerPoint, ePub, Google Docs, Slides and Sheets, images, audio, public web pages, public YouTube videos, pasted text, notes, Play Books ebooks and Gemini chats; each source holds up to **500,000 words or 200 MB**, with Sheets capped at 100k tokens and Slides at 100 slides ([Add or discover sources](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en)). Everything is reduced to text. Audio "is transcribed at the time of import and its text is saved," web pages import HTML text only, and YouTube imports only the caption transcript; Drive footnotes and comments are not imported, and certain image types "may not work as well" (same page). Transcription is lossy, as Raiza Martin conceded: "we don't transcribe, like, the emotion" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)). Drive sources now auto-sync every few minutes, whereas an earlier university guide described manual re-sync ([New School KB](https://services.newschool.edu/TDClient/32/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=1371)); the date of the change is unknown. A stale or inaccessible Drive source stays counted but is ignored, a graceful-degradation pattern worth copying. The Enterprise edition stores "a static copy" of each upload and converts spreadsheets into an intermediate format whose structure characters count toward the word limit ([Enterprise docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/overview)).

Inside, the evidence is reverse-engineered. The backend stores each source as a typed structured document whose elements (paragraph, table with rows and cells, image, code) carry start and end indices ([chat.proto](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/src/notebooklm/_android/proto_src/google/internal/labs/tailwind/orchestration/v1/chat.proto)); `GetSource` returns "clean fulltext" with a word count; and a `RetrieveRelevantChunks` RPC returns "globally ranked source passages" as text, rank and spans in the source's coordinate space ([source-search-evidence](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/android/source-search-evidence.md)). A ranked-chunk index therefore exists, so embeddings or an equivalent are almost certainly built at ingest (*inference*); chunk size, embedding model and OCR engine are undocumented. Image and scanned-PDF reading is attributed to Gemini's native vision: Martin said "if you have a PDF that's purely images, it will recognize it" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)). No independent test of Notebook's table, scan or figure fidelity exists; an HN comment that it "can now manage ocr/htr" is an anecdote ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48750605)).

Ingestion is asynchronous. The client library shows a source row appearing at once and then moving through PREPARING to READY or ERROR, so "row exists" and "usable" are different states; failed adds can leave "ghost" rows that inflate the count, HTML uploads are rejected, X.com pages ingest as error pages, and files over about 20 MB can time out on the consumer web path ([troubleshooting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/troubleshooting.md), [mcp-guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/mcp-guide.md)). Import of Deep Research results is not atomic: a timeout commits part of the batch and a retry fails with FAILED_PRECONDITION ([rpc-reference](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/rpc-reference.md)). Each source also receives a **source guide**. In 2023 Steven Johnson described a summary, key topics and three suggested questions per source ([Johnson](https://adjacentpossible.substack.com/p/introducing-notebooklm)); today's help pages confirm an auto-generated summary and a notebook-level summary with suggested options. The reverse-engineered `DocumentGuide{snippet, main_ideas}`, key-topic chips that launch chat queries and `NextStepSuggestions` of three question chips match that ([sources.proto](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/src/notebooklm/_android/proto_src/google/internal/labs/tailwind/orchestration/v1/sources.proto)). Nothing in Google's statements or the traffic shows the guide feeding retrieval or routing; the evidenced function is orientation, which removes the blank-page problem and raises perceived quality independent of retrieval (*inference*).

## Answer quality stacks a closed-corpus contract, pointer citations and long context

### Google's pipeline statements describe a hybrid without naming a threshold

Google has never published a pipeline architecture; the first-party material is blogs, help pages and team remarks, which trace four eras. In 2023 the Tailwind prototype was, in Raiza Martin's words, "basically rag" because context was "probably, like, 2K, 4K" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)). In May 2024 the product moved to Gemini 1.5 Pro and gained inline citations that "take you directly to supporting passages" ([Google blog, June 2024](https://blog.google/technology/ai/notebooklm-goes-global-support-for-websites-slides-fact-check/)). Johnson wrote that the window grew from about 1,500 words to 1.5 million and, "using various other tricks, effectively fit 25 million words," without naming the tricks ([Johnson](https://adjacentpossible.substack.com/p/in-the-context-of-long-context)); Martin said a 50-source, 500,000-word notebook cannot be "jam[med]" into the window ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)). On 29 October 2025 Google enabled "the full 1 million token context window of Gemini in NotebookLM chat across all plans" and said chat "automatically explores your sources from multiple angles," adding that for very large notebooks "careful context engineering is critical," with a self-reported 50% satisfaction gain on responses using many sources ([Google blog, Oct 2025](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/notebooklm-custom-personas-engine-upgrade/)). Some summaries recast that post as "query expansion"; it does not say so. In June 2026 the system moved to Gemini 3.5 with a per-notebook cloud computer and reported only relative win rates against its prior system (over 65% on average, 69.9% on large-document analysis) ([Google blog, June 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/notebooklm/better-research-notebooklm/)). The single first-party sentence on retrieval is in the current FAQ: "When your notebook contains many sources, Gemini Notebook retrieves the most relevant information based on your question, then builds a response from it," and a specific question helps ([Help FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en)).

From these statements the researchers infer a hybrid, and the inference is strong. A full notebook holds about 25 million words, far above any window, and Johnson's "effectively 25 million" equals 50 sources times 500,000 words, so the unnamed tricks are what make an oversized notebook usable; the 2026 FAQ states retrieval for many-source notebooks; and the October 2025 post calls context engineering critical at scale (*inference*). A moderate inference reads "8x larger" against a 1M ceiling as a prior working context near 125k tokens, so small and medium notebooks behave close to full context (*inference*). The flip threshold, chunking, dense versus sparse retrieval, reranking and top-k are documented nowhere. Reverse-engineering adds that the server assembles context: the client sends source ids and conversation history, and the browser does no retrieval ([chat.proto](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/src/notebooklm/_android/proto_src/google/internal/labs/tailwind/orchestration/v1/chat.proto)). Whether chat reuses the `RetrieveRelevantChunks` primitive is unconfirmed. Source text enters the model prompt as untrusted tokens: a 2024 prompt-injection report made the chatbot load a Markdown image that leaked private data, and Google fixed it on 18 April 2024 ([Willison on Rehberger](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Apr/16/google-notebooklm-data-exfiltration/)). The 2026 response schema also contains thought and function-call elements, so the agentic chat may search sources through tool calls rather than a fixed retrieve-then-answer step (*inference*).

### Citations are structured pointers, with the mechanism still inferred

The user-facing behaviour is documented: citations are "direct quotes, text, or images from your sources," hover shows the full quote, and selecting one jumps to the passage in context; very short sources are referenced whole, without an individual cited passage ([Use chat](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en), [Help FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en)). The reverse-engineered shape is richer. An answer carries a structured response document in which each citation holds source attribution, a fragment of typed blocks (so it can include table cells) and ranges; the decoded reference gives `source_id`, `cited_text` ("the cited source passage, verbatim"), `start_char` and `end_char` in the source's coordinate space (UTF-16 code units), a `chunk_id`, and a server `score` typically 0.6 to 0.7 ([chat types](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/src/notebooklm/_types/chat.py), [proto-evidence-ledger](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/android/proto-evidence-ledger.md)). Notes saved from chat store one descriptor per unique cited chunk ([rpc-reference](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/rpc-reference.md)).

The researchers read this as citations being pointers into the stored source, not model-written quotes (*inference*, moderate): the fragment is a typed block structure with source offsets, the unit is named `chunk_id`, and saved notes dedupe per chunk. A pointer design means a displayed passage cannot be a fabricated string even when the claim-to-passage match is wrong. The likely flow is retrieval of candidate chunks with ids, an answer that references them, server-side resolution to spans and verbatim fragments, and numbering by first appearance. The alternative, post-hoc claim-to-chunk alignment like Vertex AI's Check Grounding, which scores claims against facts with a default 0.6 citation threshold and treats partial entailment as ungrounded ([Check grounding](https://docs.cloud.google.com/generative-ai-app-builder/docs/check-grounding)), cannot be ruled out, and the matching 0.6 range is a coincidence-level hint only. Citation granularity looks chunk-sized rather than sentence-exact, since ranges can be a union of spans (*inference*, weak). Google has not said how the model emits citations, and the client library flags its multi-citation anchors as unverified.

### Refusal is documented; accuracy evidence is small and confounded

Google documents a strict contract: "If the answer isn't in the source material, it won't provide a response"; creative requests may return "can't answer"; safety filters can also block answers ([Help FAQ](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187?hl=en), [Use chat](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en)). Since June 2026 the contract is mode-dependent, because agentic chat can search the web, run code and make files. The answer message has an `EmptyAnswerReason` enum (UNANSWERABLE, FILTERED), but the client library's live probes never observed a nonzero value ([proto-evidence-ledger](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/android/proto-evidence-ledger.md)), so a separate server-side answerability signal is unproven. Google publishes no hallucination, citation-precision or recall figure at any era. The independent studies are few:

| Study | Model era and design | Result | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hagar et al., [arXiv 2509.25498](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25498v1) | Tested Jul to Aug 2025, before the Oct 2025 upgrade; 300-document TikTok corpus, five prompts | Hallucination in 13% of Notebook responses (2 of 15) vs 40% for ChatGPT GPT-5 Projects (6 of 15) and 40% for Gemini 2.5 Pro with ~923k tokens in context (4 of 10) | Methods and Results counts conflict; one topic, one annotator; authors call it "directional"; ChatGPT Projects capped at 100 documents |
| Lung-cancer staging, [arXiv 2410.10869](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10869v1) | Gemini 1.5 Pro; 100 fictional cases against a guideline summary | 86% vs 39% (GPT-4o with guideline in prompt) and 25% (without); cited reference locations judged appropriate in 95% | Fictional cases, one guideline; main error was numerical comparison |
| IBD systematic-review extraction, [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.16.26355773v1.full) | Gemini 3, extraction on 26 Jan 2026; 57 articles, 1,710 cells | 91.17% cell accuracy; 4 cells (0.23%) major factual errors; errors mainly minor omission (90) and incomplete extraction (46) | Preprint; no citation audit; no gold subset |
| Dental-trauma guideline questions, [abstract](https://repositori.uic.es/handle/20.500.12328/5238) | 99 yes/no questions; model era not stated | 92.93% accuracy and 100% consistency vs 83.83% and 74.74% for standalone Gemini; accuracy gap not significant (p = 0.076) | Abstract only; notebook size unverified |

The pattern across the studies is a failure mode of recall and interpretation, not invention. The authors of the IBD study call the principal limitation "insufficient detail extraction rather than generation of incorrect information"; Hagar et al. report "interpretive overconfidence" and attribution drift in both retrieval and in-context setups; the staging study reports numerical-comparison errors. That fits Google's own advice that specific questions help a many-source notebook (*inference*, moderate). Hagar et al. also found NotebookLM beat Gemini 2.5 Pro given the same documents in context, which is consistent with retrieval plus citation beating raw stuffing for synthesis, but the tools differ in prompts and instructions, so the cause is confounded.

### Ranking the reasons quality feels high

No ablation exists, so the ranking below is an evidence-weighted judgement, not a causal result. First, the closed-corpus, refuse-if-absent contract is firmly documented, and the 13 versus 40% and 86 versus 25 to 39% gaps are consistent with it, though confounded with citations and retrieval design. Second, enforced passage-level citations over a structured source store are documented in behaviour and reverse-engineered in structure; Johnson's description of the experience, "ask a complicated question, get a nuanced answer... and click through the citations to confirm," names the trust loop ([Johnson](https://adjacentpossible.substack.com/p/in-the-context-of-long-context)). Third, a strong long-context model with the full 1M window since October 2025 is documented, with the 50% satisfaction gain self-reported. Fourth, retrieval and context engineering for big notebooks is documented only at the level of two sentences, and Google's 69.9% large-document win rate implies large notebooks were a known weak point before 2026 (*inference*). Fifth, user-curated, user-scoped and typically small corpora; sixth, ingest-time structuring; seventh, a groundedness-focused eval loop ("consistency, groundedness" in the audio team's metrics, [Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)). For a typical small-notebook user the first three carry the weight (*inference*). The owner's premise that Notebook's RAG is uniquely good is only weakly corroborated independently: the supportable claim is that closed-corpus, citation-enforced tools show lower measured hallucination than open chat given the same documents, and no study compares Notebook with Claude Projects or Perplexity on a shared corpus.

## Studio runs one recipe across ten formats, and grounding varies by format

Every Studio generator takes the selected sources plus options and produces a persisted artifact. The reverse-engineered `CREATE_ARTIFACT` call takes nested source-id lists plus instructions, format and language, and no question; artifacts record their `source_ids` and `generation_prompt`, move through statuses that include "suggested" and "pending review", and a failed one is re-queued in place with the same id, although the schema holds no failure reason ([rpc-reference](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/rpc-reference.md)). The documented user-side recipe matches: choose sources, a preset, length or detail, language and a free-text focus prompt, generate in the background, and reopen the result with "View custom prompt" ([Create a notebook](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16206563?hl=en)). That recipe is a clean template for output recipes in another platform (*inference*).

Grounding is documented unevenly. Google states that all Studio content is AI-generated and may contain errors, that flashcards come only from the user's sources, and that slide revisions ignore sources, and it documents citations on the second tab of an exported data table. Whether citations survive into reports, audio or mind maps is not documented, and neither is how a notebook larger than the window is digested for a Studio job; the request carries source ids rather than a retrieval query, so generators probably read the selected sources directly, possibly condensed first (*inference*, moderate).

The audio pipeline has the best evidence. Johnson described a staged process on the Hard Fork podcast: outline, revision, detailed script, critique, revision, and a final stage that adds "disfluencies," which "turns out to be crucial because you cannot listen to two robots talking" ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/29/notebooklm-audio-overview/)). Usama Bin Shafqat said text-to-audio "lives as a separate engineering pipeline" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/notebooklm)), and DeepMind describes a dialogue speech model that takes a script with speaker-turn markers and generates audio in a single autoregressive pass ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/pushing-the-frontiers-of-audio-generation/)). Reddit claims about the hosts' hidden prompt are unverified. Audio quality therefore comes from the editorial script stages and a purpose-built voice model, and groundedness from those stages and from evals, not from inline citations (*inference*). Video is constrained by cost: Martin said limited video output is "just a cost problem" ([Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/training-data-google-notebooklm)). The October 2026 "Review" step, which shows an outline or producer notes before rendering, answers that cost and control problem (*inference*). Unreleased Canvas, Lit Review and "App" tiles found in client code are third-party reporting and unconfirmed ([recap](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/9176/gemini-notebook-app-artifact-interactive-apps)).

## The official API provisions but cannot query, and the unofficial clients carry Terms risk

### What Google officially exposes

There is no consumer API. The only official programmatic surface is the Gemini Notebook Enterprise REST API, in **v1alpha Preview** under the Discovery Engine host, which covers notebooks (create, get, list recently viewed, batch delete, share), sources (batch create from Drive, text, web or YouTube; raw-byte upload; get; batch delete) and a single audio overview per notebook (create, delete) ([Notebooks API](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/api-notebooks), [Sources API](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/api-notebooks-sources), [Audio overview API](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/api-audio-overview)). The docs list no chat, query, video, mind-map, report, slide, quiz or note endpoints, and the audio page sends the user to the UI to download. A separate standalone Podcast API (text and files in, MP3 out, under 100,000 tokens) is Deprecated and closed to new customers ([Podcast API](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/podcast-api)). The official surface can feed an enterprise notebook but cannot ask it a question or fetch a citation (*inference* from the documented method list; absence is "not documented," not proven absent). I found no official MCP server, ADK tool or A2A agent for Notebook; the Gemini Enterprise relationship is a Public Preview in which notebooks can be searched as a data source ([Overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/overview), [Choose product](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/choose-product)).

### The unofficial ecosystem and the data model it exposes

One project dominates: teng-lin/notebooklm-py has 19.9k stars, was pushed on 27 October 2026, is pre-1.0, and ships a library, CLI, agent skill, REST server and a 38-tool MCP server; jacob-bd/gemini-notebook-mcp-cli has 6.4k stars and 53 MCP tools; tmc/nlm (Go) and roomi-fields/notebooklm-mcp are smaller ([GitHub API listing](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=notebooklm+in:name,description&sort=stars&order=desc&per_page=30)). PleasePrompto's browser-automation MCP and skill (3.4k and 7.8k stars) were archived on 10 September 2026 with no reason stated ([PleasePrompto](https://github.com/PleasePrompto/notebooklm-mcp)). All consumer-side clients log in with Google session cookies and call private `batchexecute` RPCs identified by six-character ids that Google can rotate without notice, plus a separate streaming chat endpoint; the maintainers treat id rotations as patch releases and run a nightly health check, and mark the RPC, MCP and REST surfaces unstable ([stability](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/stability.md)).

Through these clients the data model is visible (*reverse-engineered*). A notebook holds sources (twenty type codes including Gemini chat, type 18), notes, mind maps, typed artifacts with statuses, labels, chat conversations with turn keys and share state; chat settings (goal and response length) are stored per notebook; sharing has `FULL_NOTEBOOK` and `CHAT_ONLY` view levels ([rpc-reference](https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/blob/main/docs/rpc-reference.md), [python-api](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/python-api.md)). The lifecycle is polled and has three distinct readiness states: a source row registered, a source ready, and an artifact complete with its media URL populated, since media rows can be marked complete before the URL exists and links expire within hours. The clients' default wait budgets are 20 minutes for audio, 30 for video and 60 for Cinematic video, against one to two minutes for text-like artifacts; no source quantifies how long ingestion takes to reach READY. Weaknesses to avoid copying include reads with side effects (fetching a notebook updates `last_viewed_at`), artifact failures with no reason, only the latest conversation exposed, silent scope drift and non-atomic imports. Export is partial: reports go to Docs and tables to Sheets, per-artifact downloads exist, and an admin Data Export tool covers chat history, audio overviews, uploads and notes ([Export NotebookLM data](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/migrate/export-notebooklm-data)); no whole-notebook round-trip format is documented. Whether Takeout includes Notebook was not found.

### Terms-of-Service exposure and agent-pattern failure modes

Google's Terms prohibit "reverse engineering our services or underlying technology... to extract trade secrets or other proprietary information, except as allowed by applicable law," "using automated means to access content... in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages," and "bypassing our systems or protective measures," and allow suspension or termination for material or repeated breach ([Google Terms](https://policies.google.com/terms)). The clients' own READMEs say the same in practice: jacob-bd warns use "may not align with Google's Terms of Service" and suggests personal or experimental use; roomi-fields warns "automation may be detected; use a dedicated Google account"; notebooklm-py advises a dedicated account for unattended use and says heavy usage may be throttled ([jacob-bd](https://github.com/jacob-bd/gemini-notebook-mcp-cli), [roomi-fields](https://github.com/roomi-fields/notebooklm-mcp), [security](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/security.md)). The research found no ban reports specific to Notebook automation, but an adjacent precedent: from mid-February 2026, developers reported mass 403 Terms suspensions for Gemini API, Gemini CLI and Antigravity access through third-party wrappers ([forum thread](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/urgent-mass-403-tos-bans-on-gemini-api-antigravity-for-open-source-cli-users-paid-tier/124508)). Whether a given client breaches the clauses is a contract-interpretation question the research did not settle. Operationally, Google also gates by network: VPN or datacenter IPs can be redirected to an "unsupported location" page ([troubleshooting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/troubleshooting.md)).

Agents reach Notebook through a skill that shells out to a CLI or a local MCP server, and the failure modes are operational. Cookies last weeks but `__Secure-1PSIDTS` rotates on Google's schedule, competing sessions sign each other out (an exported snapshot was superseded within about 10 minutes when another client used the session), RPC ids drift, quotas shift without notice (Free-tier Infographics and Slide Decks were briefly set to 0 in November 2025), instructions passed to chat are advisory while only `source_ids` is a hard filter, and a 53-tool MCP consumes large context ([auth-cookie-lifecycle](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/auth-cookie-lifecycle.md), [quota-limits](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/quota-limits.md), [mcp-guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/docs/mcp-guide.md)). The best-maintained clients add confirm gates on destructive tools, background chat start, status and cancel, short-lived signed links and replay-safety classes for retries, all worth copying into an adapter that can be switched off.

### The nearest official building blocks

No single official API reproduces persisted sources, server-side chat and typed artifacts. For the grounded-answer core the closest blocks are Anthropic Citations (documents you supply; sentence-level chunking; `char_location`, `page_location` and `content_block_location` payloads; pointers "guaranteed to contain valid pointers"; incompatible with structured outputs) ([Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)); Gemini API File Search (a persistent store with configurable chunking, metadata filters and `file_citation` annotations that include page numbers, 100 MB per document) ([File Search](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/file-search)); and OpenAI file search (vector stores with file-level citations only) ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-file-search)). Server-held chat with notes and typed Studio artifacts have no equivalent in any of them. A durable design holds source text, chunks and citation spans itself and pushes copies into Notebook only when Studio outputs such as audio, video or slides are wanted (*inference*).

## Analogues and literature favour routed hybrid retrieval with span-level citations

### What the open-source clones build, and where they fall short

Only three analogues reproduce the notebook loop of source scope, cited chat and generated artifacts. Open Notebook (40.6k stars, MIT) sends selected content to chat in full with no retrieval, offers three context levels per source (Not included, Insights only, Full content), and routes cross-source questions through a separate Ask path that plans up to five searches (top 10 each), drafts partial answers and merges them; chunks are about 400 tokens with 15% overlap, and prompts above about 105,000 tokens switch to a large-context model ([AI Context and RAG](https://github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook/blob/main/docs/2-CORE-CONCEPTS/ai-context-rag.md)). Its own citation documentation concedes the gap that matters most: "A citation always points to a whole item," carries no page or highlighted passage, and "can be missing or point to the wrong item" ([Citations](https://github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook/blob/main/docs/3-USER-GUIDE/citations.md)). Its default extractor "loses table structure and layout; no OCR" unless Docling is enabled ([content processing](https://github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook/blob/main/docs/3-USER-GUIDE/content-processing-engines.md)). An independent hands-on review found the features close but setup slow, podcasts shorter by default and quality slightly weaker, and did not evaluate citations ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/notebooklm-alternative-open-notebook-hands-on-3677932/)). SurfSense (16.6k stars) runs BM25 and vector legs of 20 candidates each blended 0.65 semantic to 0.35 keyword, with no reranker ([ADR 0006](https://github.com/MODSetter/SurfSense/blob/main/docs/adr/0006-hybrid-retrieval.md)). Kotaemon (25.8k stars, slower activity) offers relevance-scored citations with highlights in an in-browser PDF viewer ([Kotaemon](https://github.com/Cinnamon/kotaemon)). AnythingLLM, Open WebUI, Dify, RAGFlow and PrivateGPT are components or baselines, not notebooks. Among commercial rivals, Claude Projects switches to retrieval automatically as knowledge nears the window and claims "response accuracy remains consistent with in-context processing," a vendor claim with an undisclosed threshold ([Claude help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11473015-retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-for-projects)).

The most transferable analogue lesson is about measurement. SurfSense's earlier design was "written without a way to measure it"; once it built a retrieval eval over 266 queries, an answering passage reached the prompt for only 50% of queries under cosine ordering, 88% under reciprocal rank fusion, and 98% (mean rank 1.1 versus 3.7) under a weighted blend, on one internal corpus with a small embedder ([ADR 0031](https://github.com/MODSetter/SurfSense/blob/main/docs/adr/0031-ranking-blends-absolute-leg-scores.md)). The gain came from measuring, not from a new technique. No analogue publishes citation precision or an answer-quality comparison against Notebook.

### What retrieval benchmarks support

Evidence is strongest for hybrid dense plus sparse retrieval with a reranker over a deep candidate pool. Anthropic's vendor-run contextual-retrieval study cut top-20 failure from 5.7% to 3.7% with contextual embeddings, 2.9% adding contextual BM25 and 1.9% adding a reranker over the top 150 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). An independent 2026 benchmark of 23,088 financial queries found a two-stage hybrid-plus-neural-rerank pipeline beat every single-stage method (Recall@5 0.816), BM25 beat dense retrieval on those documents, and HyDE and multi-query helped little on precise numerical queries ([arXiv 2604.01733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01733)); a secondary summary of the same paper puts Recall@5 at 0.458 with 20 reranked candidates and 0.888 with 100, a figure I could not verify against the paper. Structured alternatives pay off on broad questions at a price: GraphRAG-Bench shows vanilla RAG tying graph methods on fact retrieval while graph methods win on complex reasoning and summarisation, at tokens per query of 879 for vanilla RAG, 3,441 for RAPTOR and 100,832 for LightRAG ([arXiv 2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v3)). Late interaction helps visual-document retrieval, with no notebook-style text benchmark showing it beats hybrid plus rerank. Agentic search is a retrieval policy whose edge depends on the harness: grep beat vector retrieval in a conversational-memory study but fell from 93% to 55% when results arrived as files ([arXiv 2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184), numbers from a secondary summary), and an agentic-RAG baseline did not beat plain RAG on multi-document synthesis ([arXiv 2603.08329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08329)).

### Long context against retrieval at notebook scale

The evidence says route, not choose. In Google's LOFT study (Gemini 1.5 Pro, 128k), long context and a specialised RAG pipeline tied on average (0.53 against 0.52); long context won NQ, HotPotQA and MuSiQue, retrieval won the list-style QAMPARI and QUEST, and long context degraded at 1M tokens ([LOFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13121v1)). LaRA (ICML 2025, 11 models) found long context ahead of RAG by 2.40 points at 32k and behind by 3.68 at 128k, strongly ahead on comparison tasks (+15.22 at 32k) and far behind on hallucination detection (-10.38 at 32k, -22.36 at 128k), because long-context models tended to fabricate answers when the answer was absent; stronger proprietary models still favoured long context at 128k and weaker open models favoured RAG ([LaRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09977v1)). NoLiMa found 11 of 13 long-context models below half their short-context baseline at 32k ([NoLiMa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)), and Chroma's "Context Rot" study found degradation with input length in all 18 models tested ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)). Self-Route showed that routing each query to RAG or long context by model self-reflection cuts cost while keeping long-context performance ([arXiv 2407.16833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833)). On the LOONG multi-document benchmark, full context scored 68.0, ordinary RAG 33.0, agentic RAG 32.8 and one-agent-per-document map-reduce 58.1 at 38% of full-context cost, though that is the proposing authors' own method ([arXiv 2603.08329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08329)). Vendor guidance converges: Anthropic says a knowledge base under about 200,000 tokens can simply go in the prompt ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)), and Open WebUI recommends full-context mode for single documents under about 50 pages ([Open WebUI](https://docs.openwebui.com/troubleshooting/rag)).

Three implications follow for a notebook. A 20-page paper is roughly 10 to 15k tokens, so 50 to 100 sources land between 0.5M and 1.5M tokens, the edge of the 1M window, and Notebook's hundreds of sources of up to 500,000 words each cannot be served by raw full context (*inference*, the researchers' estimate). Absent-answer detection is exactly where long context is weakest in LaRA, which makes Notebook's refuse-if-absent contract hard to meet by stuffing alone and suggests a separate answerability signal or retrieval check, although the `UNANSWERABLE` enum was never seen populated (*inference*). And cross-document synthesis is limited by evidence coverage, not reading ability, so per-source summaries, map-reduce or full context beat naive top-k retrieval (*inference* from one paper). A router that uses full context with prompt caching, whose reads are priced at a tenth of base input ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)), when the selected scope fits, and per-source summaries plus hybrid retrieval beyond it, is consistent with every product and study above.

### Citation and parsing practice

The decisive choice in the citation APIs is giving the generator pre-segmented, addressable units and resolving citations deterministically to them, which removes fabricated pointers but not unsupported claims. Measured citation quality is mediocre outside vendor claims: across four generative search engines only 51.5% of sentences were fully supported by their citations and 74.5% of citations supported their sentence ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)), SourceCheckup found 50 to 90% of medical responses not fully supported by the sources they cite ([PMC12003634](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12003634/)), and NLI-based checkers agree with humans only 78 to 85% of the time ([ALCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)), so a platform needs a human-labelled sample to calibrate any automatic faithfulness metric. Anthropic recommends quote-first extraction for long documents, retracting claims with no supporting quote, and allowing "I don't know" ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations)). On parsing, no neutral head-to-head covers Docling, Marker, MinerU, Mistral OCR, LlamaParse and Gemini or Claude vision on the same documents. The vendor-run ParseBench found most specialised parsers below 6% on charts and Docling at 1.0% on semantic formatting against 85.2% for LlamaParse Agentic ([LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/parsebench)); because benchmarks disagree on the winner, the supportable move is to run candidate parsers on the owner's documents and score downstream retrieval (*inference*).

## The reproducible core is firm; the pipeline's tuning stays unknown

### What to reproduce, ordered by how firm the evidence is

| Mechanism | Evidence and firmness | What the evidence supports building |
|---|---|---|
| Closed corpus with refuse-if-absent and distinct "not in sources" and "filtered" outcomes | Documented, firm; effect measured only in small, confounded studies | A source-only mode with a refusal path, kept apart from any web or code agent mode, as Notebook now does |
| Per-query source scope as a hard filter | Documented in the UI, reverse-engineered as an id list; firm | Scope as an explicit argument, stored with each answer to prevent drift |
| Citation as a pointer to a stored span with verbatim fragment, offsets and score | Shape reverse-engineered, firm; "pointer, not quote" is inference | A span-addressed source store, ids resolved server-side, so displayed passages cannot be invented |
| Structured source documents (typed blocks, offsets, tables) | Reverse-engineered, firm for existence; parse quality unmeasured | Parse to blocks with offsets; choose parsers by testing on your own documents |
| Full context when the scope fits, retrieval or map-reduce beyond | Direction documented and supported by literature; threshold unknown | A router with a measured threshold and an answerability check |
| Per-source guide at ingest | Documented, firm; no evidence it aids retrieval | Orientation and suggested questions; optionally an "insights only" tier |
| Notes as citation-preserving, promotable-to-source answers | Documented, firm | Save an answer with its span descriptors, then promote it to a source |
| Studio recipe and artifact state machine | Parameters documented; statuses reverse-engineered | Source ids, preset, length, language, focus, async states, retry in place, a stored failure reason |
| Audio as outline, script, critique, disfluency stages | Interview quotes, moderate; prompts unverified | Replicate the script pipeline; the voice model is the hard part |
| Measurement harness | SurfSense's 50% to 98% gain on its own corpus; Google evals are relative only | A labelled query set built before any tuning |
| Notebook as backend | Reverse-engineered, unstable, Terms-exposed | An adapter target on a dedicated account, never the system of record |

### What nobody has measured

The pipeline's tuning is the largest gap: no source states chunk size, overlap, embedding model, reranking, top-k, the full-context-to-retrieval threshold, how the model emits citations, or whether chat uses the same chunks as `RetrieveRelevantChunks`. No ablation isolates which factor causes the quality, and no study audits citation precision or recall at scale, tests recall in large notebooks, or compares Notebook with Claude Projects or Perplexity on a shared corpus. Google's win rates are relative to its own prior system, and the independent studies span Gemini 1.5 Pro, Gemini 2.5 and Gemini 3, none the current Gemini 3.5 agentic system; the one comparative study also has internally inconsistent counts. Studio is similarly dark: how oversized notebooks are digested, how video, slide and mind-map pipelines work, whether artifacts regenerate when sources change, and artifact retention are undocumented. On the platform side, no independent parse-fidelity test, no frontier-model benchmark of multi-document synthesis with citations at 100k to 1M tokens, and no Notebook-specific ban reports or chat-latency figures were found. Community evidence is thin: several key transcripts (Lenny's Podcast, Hard Fork, the DeepMind podcast) were not retrievable, and Hacker News and blog searches returned single-line guesses such as "probably RAG plus large contexts" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42354409)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what is worth copying. The best-supported reading (no ablation proves it) is that Notebook's perceived quality comes not from a secret retriever but from a contract enforced end to end: a closed corpus, a refusal path, and citations that resolve to stored spans, sitting on a long-context model that lets small notebooks run close to full context. That contract is documented or firmly reverse-engineered, cheap to build, and independent of anything Google has kept private. The tuned part, the retrieval and context engineering that Google itself says matters for large notebooks, is the part no one has measured, so copying it by imitation is impossible and the only route is to build a measurement harness first and let your own corpus set the full-context threshold.

The product's best-evidenced weakness is also the opening for a platform that owns its own sources: isolation, with no cross-notebook query, thin export and a notes-to-sources loop as the only compounding path. A design that keeps source text and spans in its own store, treats notes as promotable citation-bearing sources, queries across scopes, and uses Notebook only as an expendable Studio renderer behind an adapter can exceed the product on the dimension reviewers criticise most, while avoiding both the Terms exposure and the RPC drift that make the unofficial clients a poor foundation.
